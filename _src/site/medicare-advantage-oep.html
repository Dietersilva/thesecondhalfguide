The other Medicare enrollment window, in January

Fall’s Annual Enrollment Period isn’t the only chance to change Medicare coverage. A second, narrower window opens every January — but only for people already on Medicare Advantage, and only once.

By Edward Silva · 5 minute read · Verified against CMS enrollment rules

Most of what gets said about changing Medicare coverage points to the fall — the Annual Enrollment Period, October 15 through December 7. That is the big one, but it is not the only one. A second, considerably narrower window opens every January, and it is easy to confuse with the first.

The two windows, side by side
Annual Enrollment Period (AEP)October 15 – December 7. Open to everyone on Medicare. Switch between Original Medicare and Medicare Advantage in either direction, change Advantage plans, change Part D plans.
Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period (MA OEP)January 1 – March 31. Open only to people already enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan on January 1.
What MA OEP allowsOne change: switch to a different Medicare Advantage plan, or drop Advantage entirely and return to Original Medicare (picking up a standalone Part D plan at the same time, if returning to Original Medicare).
What it does not allowYou cannot use MA OEP to switch into Medicare Advantage from Original Medicare, and you cannot use it to change a standalone Part D plan while staying on Original Medicare.
One change onlyUnlike AEP, which allows changing your mind more than once before it ends, MA OEP permits a single plan change during the window.

Why the two get confused

Both are commonly called “Medicare open enrollment” in casual conversation, which is exactly the source of the confusion. AEP is the broad one, open to anyone with Medicare, that most marketing and most news coverage is actually about. MA OEP is a narrower do-over, specifically for people who used AEP (or any other window) to land on a Medicare Advantage plan and, within the first few months of the year, decide it isn’t working for them.

A common real scenario: someone switches to a new Medicare Advantage plan during AEP in the fall, the plan takes effect January 1, and within weeks they discover a doctor they need is out of network or a drug isn’t covered the way they expected. MA OEP exists for exactly that — one more chance to correct course before locking in for the rest of the year.

AEP is where most people make their decision. MA OEP is where some people get to unmake it — once, and only if they were already on Medicare Advantage to begin with.

Worth knowing

  • If you are on Original Medicare as of January 1, MA OEP does not apply to you at all — it only opens for existing Medicare Advantage enrollees.
  • The change you make during MA OEP takes effect the first day of the month after the plan receives your request, not January 1 retroactively.
  • If you return to Original Medicare during MA OEP, you can pick up a standalone Part D plan at the same time — this is one of the few times you can newly enroll in Part D outside AEP without a separate qualifying event.
  • Returning to Original Medicare here does not reopen your one-time Medigap guaranteed-issue window unless you separately qualify for a protection under your state’s rules or a federal trial-right provision.
  • Because it is one change only, use it deliberately — this is not a second AEP to shop around in, it is a single correction.

Two windows, seven months apart, serving different purposes — one broad and available to everyone with Medicare, the other narrow and available only to undo a Medicare Advantage decision that already didn’t work out. Confusing which one applies to you is the easiest way to miss both.
