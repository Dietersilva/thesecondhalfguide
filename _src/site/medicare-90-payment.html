The $90 Medicare payment: who gets it and who doesn’t

CMS announced a one-time $90 rebate for certain people in Original Medicare Part B, scheduled for October 2026. Eligibility and missing-payment questions go to different agencies.

By Edward Silva · 5 minute read · Checked against CMS’s FAQ, Medicare.gov and independent reporting that cites them

CMS announced a one-time $90 Medicare Improvement Fund rebate for eligible Original Medicare Part B beneficiaries, scheduled for October 2026. The agency described about 20.8 million people as eligible. Its timetable placed most direct deposits around October 8, with Treasury checks later that month.

The rebate is real, it is small, and it has one feature that impersonators can exploit: you do not apply for it. An automatic rebate can give a caller a believable story, a demand to pay a fee or hand over information to “release” it.

The payment, at a glance
Amount$90, one time.
SourceThe Medicare Improvement Fund, per CMS’s FAQ. Separate from your monthly Social Security benefit.
Who is paidPeople in Original Medicare Part B who live in the United States. A Medigap policy or a Part D plan on top of Original Medicare does not by itself disqualify you; other conditions still apply.
Who is left outPeople in Medicare Advantage; people receiving Medicaid assistance with their Medicare premium; people who pay IRMAA (the income-related surcharge); and people living outside the U.S. Edge cases are a question for Medicare.
How manyAbout 20.8 million people, per CMS.
How it was scheduledDirect deposit, most around October 8. Otherwise a Treasury check later in October.
Do you apply?No. No application is part of the announced process.
Eligibility questionsMedicare: 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227).
Missing paymentCMS directs payment-status questions to Social Security: 1-800-772-1213, available from October 15, 2026.

Who is left out, and why that surprises people

The exclusions are worth reading slowly, because each one catches a different kind of household. (Living in the United States is also a condition of getting the payment.)

Medicare Advantage. The payment goes to people in Original Medicare Part B, so a person in an Advantage plan is not in the group, even though that person also has Part B.

Medicaid premium help. People receiving Medicaid assistance with their Medicare premium are not eligible. This is the exclusion that confuses lower-income readers, who might reasonably assume the opposite.

IRMAA. People who pay the income-related surcharge are excluded. There is no single “income cutoff” to memorize: it is the IRMAA status that decides. For 2026, the standard Part B IRMAA threshold is modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) above $109,000 for an individual return or $218,000 for a joint return, generally based on 2024 tax information, as covered in the piece on IRMAA. Social Security can adjust an IRMAA determination in qualifying circumstances, such as a life-changing event or an amended return. The rebate exclusion concerns IRMAA status. It is not a separate $19,000 income test, and no such figure appears in the eligibility rules.

Eligibility depends on Medicare enrollment, residence, Medicaid premium assistance and IRMAA status.

How it was paid, and what you did not have to do

Social Security makes the payment, using the banking information it already has for you. Most people with direct deposit on file were scheduled to receive it around October 8. People without a deposit account on file were to get a paper check at the address Medicare has for them. If you moved recently and your Medicare address is out of date, that is the situation in which a check goes astray. Medicare says address changes are made through Social Security; railroad beneficiaries use the Railroad Retirement Board. The steps are on Medicare’s change-of-address page.

No application is part of the announced rebate process. An unsolicited request for a fee or bank details to release it is a warning sign. If you contact an agency yourself about a missing payment, it may need to verify your identity. That is different from a stranger asking for the same details.

The scam that arrives with it

Any benefit announced to more than twenty million people is an opening for a call. The pattern is predictable: a text, an email or a phone call says the payment is “held” or “pending,” and that to release it you must confirm a bank account, a Medicare number or a Social Security number, or pay a small processing fee. None of that is part of the announced process. CMS’s FAQ says the paper check carries the memo “Medicare Improvement Fund Payment; $90 Payment to Offset October Premium.” Matching words alone do not prove that a check or a caller is genuine.

The same instinct covers the reverse case. If someone tells you that you have been “approved for extra” and need to act before a deadline, the absence of any application process is your answer. The longer piece on why your phone rings more in October covers the season’s other scripts.

If you expected it and it did not come

The announced October timetable has passed. First check whether one of the exclusions applies (a Medicare Advantage plan, Medicaid premium help, IRMAA) and whether the deposit or mailing information on file is current. For rebate eligibility, call 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227). For a missing payment or its delivery status, CMS directs beneficiaries to Social Security at 1-800-772-1213. The FAQ describes a one-time rebate; it does not establish that every payment has arrived.

The payment is separate from your monthly Social Security benefit and does not replace or change it. It is also separate from the monthly Part B premium schedule. Use numbers you looked up yourself, not one in a text or a voicemail.

What to check

  • Which kind of Medicare you have: Original Medicare with Part B is in; a Medicare Advantage plan is out.
  • Whether Medicaid helps pay your Medicare premium, or you pay IRMAA. Either one means no payment.
  • Whether the deposit or mailing address Social Security and Medicare have for you is current.
  • If you expected the rebate and it has not arrived, use the official contacts: 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227) for eligibility and Social Security, 1-800-772-1213, for payment status.
  • Do not send banking information in response to an unsolicited message.

The payment is a small, one-time event. What is worth carrying forward is the shape of it: a real benefit, an automatic one, and a simple rule for spotting anyone pretending otherwise.
