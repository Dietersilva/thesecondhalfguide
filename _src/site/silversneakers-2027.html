SilverSneakers and Medicare gym benefits for 2027: what to check before 7 December

Original Medicare does not pay for a gym. Some Medicare Advantage plans do, in different programs under different names, and the benefit is decided plan by plan, every year.

By Edward Silva · 5 minute read · Checked against plan announcements, SilverSneakers and independent reporting

“Free gym membership with Medicare” is one of the most searched phrases of the fall, and it is both true and misleading. It is true for many people with a Medicare Advantage plan. It is not true of Medicare itself. The difference is why a benefit that was there in 2026 can be gone in 2027 with no change to anything the person did.

Annual Enrollment runs through 7 December. Here is what is known about the gym benefit, what is not, and where to look.

Medicare gym benefits, in numbers
Original MedicareParts A and B do not cover gym memberships or fitness programs.
Plan by planFitness benefits are add-ons to individual Medicare Advantage plans. They can be added, changed or removed each year.
About 9 in 10Share of Medicare Advantage plans offering some fitness benefit in 2026, down slightly from 2025. Reports citing KFF give 93% and 91%.
15 October – 7 DecemberAnnual Enrollment, when a plan can be changed for 2027.

Plan documentsThe 2027 Summary of Benefits and Evidence of Coverage control what is provided. The Annual Notice of Change summarizes the differences.

What SilverSneakers is

SilverSneakers is a fitness program that a health plan buys on behalf of its members, who then get access to participating gyms and classes at no extra cost. It is not a Medicare benefit. It is a product that some plans license, in the same way that other plans use their own programs. The best known alternative is UnitedHealthcare’s Renew Active, which is reported to appear on certain UnitedHealthcare Medicare Advantage and AARP Medicare Supplement plans. A member who sees a different name on a 2027 notice has not necessarily lost the benefit. The program name changed.

That is the first thing to check, because the names are not interchangeable. Someone who has used a particular gym through SilverSneakers needs to know whether that gym is part of the plan’s new program, and not just whether “a fitness benefit” is listed.

What is known about 2027

Aetna has said it will continue to offer a SilverSneakers basic fitness membership to individual Medicare Advantage members at no additional cost. Beyond that, no industry-wide announcement of drops turned up, and the reporting is mixed. Some plans have dropped SilverSneakers but offer a similar benefit in its place. Others have dropped fitness benefits altogether. The overall figure shows how small the shift is in aggregate and how large it can be for one person: about nine in ten Advantage plans offered some fitness benefit in 2026, and a few points fewer than the year before.

It is also not limited to Advantage plans. Some Medicare Supplement (Medigap) policies include a fitness program, though not as a standard feature, and an insurer’s own materials are the place to see whether a given policy does.

A gym benefit is part of the plan, not part of Medicare. It can change on 1 January with nothing else changing.

Where to look, in order

The Annual Notice of Change is the first place. It is mailed in the fall and lists what is changing for the coming year, including a changed or dropped benefit. It is covered on the Annual Notice of Change page. The Evidence of Coverage is the binding document, and the Summary of Benefits gives the short form. SilverSneakers itself says to ask the health plan whether the program is included going into 2027, and offers a way to check on its own site. Calling the plan and asking for the benefit by name is slower than reading a page and more reliable than a brochure.

Plan details that vary include which gyms participate, whether home fitness kits are offered, and whether the benefit comes with a card, an app or a separate sign-up. None of that carries over automatically if a person changes plans in the fall.

The timing rule people miss

Annual Enrollment is when a plan can be changed for 2027. A person who finds out in January that the gym benefit is gone has a narrower option: the Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period, 1 January to 31 March, which allows one change by someone already in an Advantage plan. One guide adds that losing a fitness benefit does not, by itself, create a special enrollment period. That is a general reporting point and not a statement about any person’s eligibility. The practical consequence is plain: the notice that arrives in the fall is the one to read in the fall. The rules for the January window are on the Advantage open enrollment page, and the broader rules for October and December are on the open enrollment page.

What a fitness benefit is, and is not

A gym benefit is one line among many in a plan, alongside the allowances covered on the flex card page. It is not a medical service, and Medicare does cover the free yearly visit that is a different thing, covered on the wellness visit page. The reason the gym benefit is worth a look is that it is decided plan by plan and can change each year. This page does not say whether a person should choose a plan for it. It says where the benefit is written down.

What’s worth keeping in mind

  • Original Medicare does not cover gym memberships. Fitness benefits belong to individual Medicare Advantage plans, and to some Medigap policies.
  • The program can change name or disappear each year. Aetna says it continues SilverSneakers basic for individual Advantage members; UnitedHealthcare uses its own Renew Active.
  • Read the Annual Notice of Change and the 2027 Evidence of Coverage; check the program name and the participating gym.
  • Annual Enrollment closes 7 December. After that, a person already in an Advantage plan has one change from 1 January to 31 March.

The benefit is easy to take for granted because it is free to use. It is only free for as long as the plan still includes it, and the place that says so is a letter most people do not open.
