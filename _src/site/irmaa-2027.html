The 2027 Medicare income surcharge starts at $112,000 single, $224,000 joint

CMS published the 2027 IRMAA brackets on 17 November. The surcharge is based on your 2025 tax return, and each step is a cliff, not a slope.

By Edward Silva · 6 minute read · Checked against the CMS fact sheet and independent reporting that cites it

Medicare charges some people more than the standard premium, based on income. The surcharge is called IRMAA, for income-related monthly adjustment amount, and it applies to both Part B and Part D. On 17 November 2026 CMS published the 2027 brackets, which take effect on 1 January 2027 and are based on the income reported on a 2025 tax return.

The standard Part B premium for 2027 is $209.40. The surcharge starts for a single filer whose modified adjusted gross income is above $112,000, and for a married couple filing jointly above $224,000. Those thresholds were $109,000 and $218,000 for 2026.

The 2027 IRMAA tiers, by 2025 modified adjusted gross income
$112,000 or less single; $224,000 or less jointNo surcharge. Part B is the standard $209.40. Part D has no surcharge.
$112,001 to $141,000 single; $224,001 to $282,000 jointPart B total $293.10 a month, which is $83.70 more. Part D surcharge $15.40.
$141,001 to $177,000 single; $282,001 to $354,000 jointPart B total $418.80, which is $209.40 more. Part D surcharge $39.70.
$177,001 to $212,000 single; $354,001 to $424,000 jointPart B total $544.40, which is $335.00 more. Part D surcharge $64.00.
$212,001 to $499,999 single; $424,001 to $749,999 jointPart B total $670.00, which is $460.60 more. Part D surcharge $88.30.
$500,000 or more single; $750,000 or more jointPart B total $711.80, which is $502.40 more. Part D surcharge $96.40.

Where the 2025 income comes in

IRMAA looks back two years. The 2027 amounts are set from the 2025 return, which Social Security receives from the IRS. If the 2025 return is not available, the agency uses the most recent one it has. The income measure is modified adjusted gross income, which is the adjusted gross income on the return plus tax-exempt interest.

That means the figure includes taxable withdrawals from traditional accounts, Roth conversions, capital gains, interest, dividends and the taxable part of Social Security. It does not include qualified withdrawals from a Roth account. A qualified charitable distribution from an IRA never enters adjusted gross income, which is why it comes up on the required withdrawals page. The rest of the rules, including how a person asks Social Security to use a more recent year after a life-changing event, are on the IRMAA page.

The surcharge is a cliff. One dollar over a threshold moves the whole premium to the next tier for the year.

The rule behind the table

The tiers are not arbitrary dollar amounts. By law the standard Part B premium covers about 25% of average Part B costs, and each IRMAA tier raises the total to a larger share of those costs: 35%, 50%, 65%, 80% and 85%. Divide each total by the standard premium and the pattern shows. The first tier is 1.4 times $209.40, and the top tier is 3.4 times it. Because the table is built from the standard premium, it moves every time that premium moves.

The Part D amounts follow a similar structure, but they are set from the national average base premium for drug coverage and are paid on top of whatever plan a person chose.

What one step costs

A single filer with 2025 income of $112,001 pays $83.70 a month more for Part B than a filer at $112,000, and $15.40 more for Part D. That is $99.10 a month, or about $1,189 over 2027. The two filers differ by one dollar of income. The same logic repeats at each threshold in the table.

Compared with 2026, the first-tier Part B surcharge rises from about $81 to $83.70, and the thresholds move up about $3,000 for a single filer and $6,000 for a joint return. The top tier total goes from $689.90 to $711.80.

The thresholds are adjusted each year for inflation and rounded to the nearest $1,000, which is why $109,000 became $112,000 for a single filer and $218,000 became $224,000 for a joint return. The top tier begins at $500,000 single and $750,000 joint. The income tax numbers that moved on a similar schedule are on the 2027 tax numbers page.

The 2027 premium reads the 2025 return, so the 2026 return that people file in the spring will set the 2028 amounts. A year in which income spikes is the one that is read two years later, and that is the sentence the IRMAA page spends most of its length on.

How the Part D surcharge is paid

The Part D amount is paid to Medicare, not to the drug plan. It is deducted from a Social Security benefit or billed directly, regardless of how the plan premium is paid, and it applies to a Medicare Advantage plan that includes drug coverage as well. The Part B surcharge is added to the Part B premium. People who do not have the premium withheld from a benefit receive a bill.

Who is not in this table

The table above covers single filers and joint filers. Married people who file separately and lived with their spouse during the year are measured against a much narrower set of brackets, and the amounts for 2027 were not confirmed in enough sources to print here. Social Security’s own notice lists the tier that applies to a given person.

The Part A premium, the Part B deductible and the way the standard premium is set are on the page on 2027 Part B costs. The earnings that the same 2025 return reports for Social Security purposes are a separate matter, covered on the earnings test page.

What to check

  • The 2027 surcharge starts above $112,000 single or $224,000 joint, measured on the 2025 return.
  • There are five tiers. Part B totals run from $293.10 to $711.80 a month, and the Part D surcharge from $15.40 to $96.40.
  • Each step is a cliff: going over a threshold by a dollar moves the whole premium.
  • The Part D surcharge goes to Medicare, not to your drug plan, and applies to Medicare Advantage plans with drug coverage too.
  • The amount that applies to you is on the notice Social Security sends, not in a table.

The table is the rule. The notice is the decision, and it is made from a tax return that was filed more than a year before the premium takes effect.
