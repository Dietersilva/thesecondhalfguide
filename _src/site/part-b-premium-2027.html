Medicare Part B costs $209.40 a month in 2027

CMS announced the 2027 amounts on 17 November: a $6.50 rise in the Part B premium, a $9 rise in the deductible, and a $1,776 hospital deductible. What it takes out of the 3.6% raise.

By Edward Silva · 6 minute read · Checked against the CMS fact sheet, the Federal Register notice and independent reporting that cites them

On 17 November 2026 CMS announced the 2027 Medicare Part A and Part B amounts. They take effect on 1 January 2027. Until then the 2026 amounts apply. The headline numbers are a standard Part B premium of $209.40 a month and a Part B deductible of $292.

This is the number that was missing from the Social Security raise. The 2027 COLA is 3.6%, and for most people the Part B premium is taken out of the payment before it arrives. Now both figures are official.

The 2027 Medicare amounts, announced 17 November 2026
$209.40The standard monthly Part B premium. Up $6.50, or 3.2%, from $202.90 in 2026.
$292The annual Part B deductible. Up $9 from $283 in 2026.
$1,776The Part A inpatient hospital deductible for each benefit period. Up $40 from $1,736.
$0The Part A premium for most people, who have at least 40 quarters of Medicare-covered work.
$328The reduced Part A premium, for people with 30 to 39 quarters. It was $311 in 2026.
$597The full Part A premium, for people with fewer than 30 quarters. It was $565 in 2026.
1 January 2027When the new amounts take effect.

How the premium is set

The Part B premium is not a negotiated price. By law it is set to cover about a quarter of the average expected cost of Part B for people on Medicare, with the rest paid from general revenue. CMS attributed this year’s increase mainly to projected growth in health care spending. The figure landed ten cents below the $209.50 that the 2026 Medicare Trustees Report had projected, which is the number the page on 2027 Medicare costs carried while the official amount was pending.

For scale: the 2026 premium rose almost 10%, from $185.00 to $202.90. The 2027 increase is about a third of that.

What it takes out of the raise

The Social Security Administration puts the average retired-worker benefit at $2,095 before the 3.6% COLA and $2,170 after, an increase of about $75 a month. The premium increase is $6.50. Subtracting one from the other gives about $68.50 a month left of the average raise for a person whose Part B premium is withheld from the benefit. That is the site’s own arithmetic on two published figures, not a number either agency reported. The premium increase absorbs roughly 9% of the average raise.

A $1,500 benefit rises by about $54, so $47.50 remains. A $3,000 benefit rises by about $108, so $101.50 remains. The premium is the same flat amount for both people, which is why it takes a larger share of a smaller raise.

The premium is a flat dollar amount. The COLA is a percentage. The two meet in the same deposit, and the flat amount comes out first.

The hold-harmless rule, and who it does not protect

A hold-harmless provision in the law limits how much of a Part B increase can reduce a person’s net Social Security payment, for people who have the premium deducted from their benefit. It matters when the premium increase is bigger than the dollar COLA. This year the premium rises $6.50, so a 3.6% raise is smaller than the increase only for a benefit under about $181 a month. For most people the provision does not limit anything in 2027.

It also does not cover everyone. People newly enrolled in Medicare, people who pay the income-related surcharge, people who do not have the premium taken from a Social Security payment, and people whose premium is paid by Medicaid are among those outside it. The mechanics are on the COLA page.

Part A: the deductible and the premium

Part A pays for inpatient hospital care, and most people do not pay a premium for it. The $1,776 deductible applies to each benefit period, which begins when you are admitted as an inpatient and ends after 60 days without inpatient care. It is not a yearly deductible, so a person admitted twice in a year, with 60 clear days between, can meet it twice. Whether a stay counts at all depends on being admitted rather than held for observation, which is covered on the observation status page.

After the deductible, hospital days 1 to 60 cost nothing. Days 61 to 90 carry a daily coinsurance of $444, which is by law one quarter of the deductible. Lifetime reserve days, of which a person has 60 over a lifetime, cost $888 a day, one half of it. Skilled nursing facility days 21 to 100 cost $222 a day, one eighth. Those three figures follow from the deductible and were checked by arithmetic. The rest of what Medicare does not pay is on the gaps page.

The Part A premium figures appear in a Federal Register notice published on 19 November. The $597 full premium is about 5.7% above 2026, and the $328 reduced premium is the full amount less 45%.

When you will see it

Social Security sends each beneficiary a notice of the new benefit amount, and the Part B premium for people who have it withheld is expected to show in that notice. People with a my Social Security account can read it online beginning in late November. Mailed notices follow in December. People who pay Medicare directly, rather than through Social Security, are billed on their own schedule.

None of this changes what a Medicare Advantage plan or a Medigap policy charges. Those premiums are set by the plan. Part B is still owed on top of them, because both are layered on Original Medicare, as the Advantage or Original page sets out.

What’s worth keeping in mind

  • The 2027 standard Part B premium is $209.40 and the deductible is $292, both effective 1 January 2027. The 2026 amounts, $202.90 and $283, apply until then.
  • The premium rose $6.50. Against the average $75 raise, about $68.50 remains for someone whose premium is withheld. That is arithmetic on published figures.
  • The Part A hospital deductible is $1,776 per benefit period. Most people pay no Part A premium; the reduced premium is $328 and the full premium is $597.
  • People who pay the income-related surcharge, are newly enrolled, or have no premium withheld are outside the hold-harmless rule.
  • Plan and Medigap premiums are set separately and are not part of this announcement.

The raise and the deposit are still different numbers, and this year the gap between them is small. The figure to check is the one on the notice that arrives with your own name on it.
