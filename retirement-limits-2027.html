The 2027 retirement account limits: the 401(k), the IRA and the catch-ups

The 401(k) limit rises $1,000 to $25,500, the catch-up for ages 60 to 63 goes to $11,750, and the IRA limit stays at $7,500. The IRS announced the 2027 amounts on 12 November.

By Edward Silva · 6 minute read · Checked against IRS Notice 2026-66 as summarized by retirement-plan and tax publications

The IRS announced next year’s retirement-plan limits on 12 November 2026, in Notice 2026-66. Most of the changes are small, and one is a number that was an estimate until this month: the wage threshold that decides whether a worker’s catch-up contributions have to go in as Roth. The limits take effect on 1 January 2027 and apply to contributions for that year.

Everything below is the published limit, not a recommendation about how much anyone should save.

The 2027 limits (2026 in parentheses)
$25,500 ($24,500)Employee deferral limit: 401(k), 403(b), eligible 457(b) plans and the Thrift Savings Plan.
$8,500 ($8,000)Catch-up for age 50 and older. Total $34,000.
$11,750 ($11,250)Catch-up for ages 60 to 63, in place of the standard one. Total $37,250.
$7,500 ($7,500)Traditional and Roth IRA contribution limit, shared across both. Unchanged.
$1,100 ($1,100)IRA catch-up for age 50 and older. Unchanged, so $8,600 in all.
$155,000 ($150,000)The prior-year FICA wage level above which workplace-plan catch-up contributions must be Roth.

The workplace-plan limits

The employee deferral limit goes from $24,500 in 2026 to $25,500. It applies to 401(k) and 403(b) plans, to eligible 457(b) plans, which include governmental and tax-exempt-employer plans, and to the federal Thrift Savings Plan. The age-based catch-ups below apply to 401(k), 403(b) and governmental 457(b) plans and the Thrift Savings Plan. Nongovernmental 457(b) plans have different special catch-up rules, and these age tiers should not be assumed to apply to them.

A worker under 50 can defer up to $25,500 in 2027. One who is 50 to 59, or 64 and older, can defer up to $34,000, which is $25,500 plus the $8,500 catch-up. One who turns 60, 61, 62 or 63 during 2027, which means a birth year from 1964 through 1967, can defer up to $37,250, which is $25,500 plus $11,750. The larger catch-up is available only if the plan allows it, as covered on the ages 60 to 63 page. Turn 64 and the amount returns to the standard catch-up. Employer matching and other contributions are separate and are not counted in these figures.

What changed, and by how much

Against 2026, the deferral limit rose by $1,000, about 4.1%. The standard catch-up rose by $500, about 6.3%, and the ages 60 to 63 catch-up by $500, about 4.4%. The Roth IRA income range for single filers moved up by $5,000 at both ends, and the joint range by $8,000. The Roth catch-up wage threshold rose by $5,000. The IRA limit and its catch-up did not move. The percentages are arithmetic on the published figures.

The IRA limits

The IRS left both IRA dollar limits unchanged for 2027: $7,500, and a $1,100 catch-up for people 50 and older, which makes $8,600 for an eligible saver. The limit is shared across a person’s traditional and Roth IRAs; it is not a separate allowance for each account. Contributions also require sufficient eligible compensation, including the spousal-IRA rules where applicable. Direct Roth IRA eligibility and traditional IRA deductibility depend on additional income and filing-status rules.

The income ranges, measured by modified adjusted gross income (MAGI), do move. For a single filer or head of household the Roth IRA range is $158,000 to $173,000, up from $153,000 to $168,000. For married couples filing jointly it is $250,000 to $260,000, up from $242,000 to $252,000. The range for deducting a traditional IRA contribution on a joint return, when the contributor is covered by a workplace plan, is $133,000 to $153,000, up from $129,000 to $149,000. When the contributor is not covered by a workplace plan but a spouse is, the joint range is $250,000 to $260,000, up from $242,000 to $252,000. For married people filing separately, the Roth range is not adjusted for inflation and stays at $0 to $10,000.

The 401(k) limit went up $1,000. The IRA limit did not move at all.

The Roth catch-up threshold

For 2027, workers whose FICA wages from the employer sponsoring the plan were more than $155,000 in 2026 generally have to make their catch-up contributions to that plan as Roth contributions, after tax. For 2026 the line was $150,000 of 2025 wages. Until this month the 2027 figure was an outside estimate, which is why the Roth catch-up page called it unconfirmed. The IRS notice settles it. The test counts wages from the plan sponsor, not self-employment income, and specified aggregation rules and later applicability dates exist for some plans.

A plan without a Roth feature generally cannot accept catch-up contributions from participants subject to the Roth catch-up requirement. It may still permit catch-ups for participants who are not subject to that requirement. This wage test concerns workplace-plan catch-ups. It does not determine eligibility to contribute to a Roth IRA.

The Saver’s Match replaces the Saver’s Credit

Starting with 2027 retirement contributions, the Saver’s Match replaces the Saver’s Credit for eligible retirement savers. Eligible individuals can receive a federal matching contribution of up to $1,000 to a designated retirement account, claimed on the 2027 return filed in 2028. The Saver’s Credit continues for eligible ABLE-account contributions. The two programs have separate eligibility rules, and the IRS page describes the income limits.

What this does not cover

It does not cover the health savings account limits, which were announced in the spring and are on the HSA page, or required minimum distributions, whose starting age depends on birth year and is on the RMD page. The income-tax numbers announced a week earlier are on their own page, and the rule that lets someone 55 or older leave an employer and draw on its plan is on the rule of 55 page.

What’s worth keeping in mind

  • The 2027 deferral limit is $25,500; the age-50 catch-up is $8,500; ages 60 to 63 get $11,750 instead. The age catch-ups apply to governmental 457(b) plans, not all 457 plans.
  • The IRA limit is $7,500, shared across traditional and Roth IRAs, plus a $1,100 catch-up. Both are unchanged.
  • The Roth catch-up threshold for 2027 is $155,000 of 2026 FICA wages from the plan sponsor.
  • The Saver’s Match replaces the Saver’s Credit for retirement contributions starting in 2027.

These are limits, not targets. Compensation, tax rules and eligibility also constrain what any one person can contribute.
