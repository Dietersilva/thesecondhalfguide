The 2027 retirement account limits: the 401(k), the IRA and the catch-ups

The 401(k) limit rises $1,000 to $25,500, the catch-up for ages 60 to 63 goes to $11,750, and the IRA limit stays at $7,500. The IRS announced the 2027 amounts on 12 November.

By Edward Silva · 6 minute read · Checked against IRS Notice 2026-66 as reported by several retirement-plan and tax outlets

The IRS announced next year’s retirement-plan limits on 12 November, in Notice 2026-66. Most of the changes are small, and one of them is a number that was an estimate until this week: the wage threshold that decides whether a worker’s catch-up contributions have to go in as Roth. These limits take effect on 1 January 2027 and apply to contributions for that year.

Everything below is the published limit, not a recommendation about how much anyone should save.

The 2027 limits, with 2026 for comparison
$25,500401(k), 403(b), most 457 plans and the federal Thrift Savings Plan. Was $24,500.
$8,500Catch-up for age 50 and older. Total $34,000. Was $8,000.
$11,750Catch-up for ages 60 to 63, in place of the standard one. Total $37,250. Was $11,250.
$7,500Traditional and Roth IRA contribution limit. Unchanged.
$1,100IRA catch-up for age 50 and older. Unchanged, so $8,600 in all.
$155,000The prior-year FICA wage level above which catch-up contributions must be Roth. Was $150,000.

The workplace-plan limits

The employee deferral limit for a 401(k), 403(b), most 457(b) plans and the Thrift Savings Plan goes from $24,500 in 2026 to $25,500. Workers 50 and older can add the standard catch-up, which rises from $8,000 to $8,500, for a total of $34,000. The larger catch-up for people who are 60, 61, 62 or 63 during the year rises from $11,250 to $11,750, for a total of $37,250. It replaces the standard catch-up for those four years and is available only if the plan allows it, as covered on the ages 60 to 63 page. Turn 64 and the amount returns to the standard figure.

What the maximum comes to, by age

The three tiers are simple to add up. A worker under 50 can defer up to $25,500 in 2027. One who is 50 to 59, or 64 and older, can defer up to $34,000, which is $25,500 plus the $8,500 catch-up. One who turns 60, 61, 62 or 63 during 2027, which means a birth year from 1964 through 1967, can defer up to $37,250, which is $25,500 plus $11,750. These are the ceilings the IRS allows. Whether a particular plan permits the full amount, or the larger catch-up at all, is the plan’s own choice. Employer matching and other contributions are separate and are not counted in these figures.

The IRA limits

The IRA limit does not change. It stays at $7,500 for 2027, and the catch-up for people 50 and older stays at $1,100, which makes $8,600 for an eligible saver. Reporting explains why: IRA limits move in $500 steps, and this year’s inflation did not carry the underlying figure far enough to cross the next one.

The income ranges that decide who can contribute to a Roth IRA, or deduct a traditional IRA contribution, do move. For a single filer or head of household the Roth range is $158,000 to $173,000, up from $153,000 to $168,000. For married couples filing jointly it is $250,000 to $260,000, up from $242,000 to $252,000. The range for deducting a traditional IRA contribution when the contributor is covered by a workplace plan, for a joint return, is $133,000 to $153,000, up from $129,000 to $149,000.

The 401(k) limit went up $1,000. The IRA limit did not move at all.

The Roth catch-up threshold

For 2027, workers whose FICA wages from the employer sponsoring the plan were more than $155,000 in 2026 generally have to make their catch-up contributions to that plan as Roth contributions, after tax. For 2026 the line was $150,000 of 2025 wages. Until this week the 2027 figure was an outside estimate, which is why the Roth catch-up page called it unconfirmed. The IRS notice settles it. The rule counts wages from the plan sponsor, not self-employment income, and a plan that offers no Roth option cannot take catch-up contributions from the workers it covers, which is the part that surprises people.

Where the 2027 numbers came from

These limits are indexed to inflation under rules set in law, and the IRS publishes them in a fall notice. The 2026 limits came out in November of last year, so the timing is normal. Outlets reporting on the notice, including the plan-sponsor trade group PSCA, a tax-accounting publication and CNBC, give the same figures for the 401(k), the catch-ups and the IRA. A few calculator sites published projections earlier in the fall, some of which differed on the IRA limit, and those are superseded by the notice.

What this does not cover

It does not cover the health savings account limits, which were announced in the spring and are on the HSA page. It does not include the Saver’s Credit thresholds, because one outlet describes a change to that credit in 2027 that the others do not mention, and the notice is the place to check. And it does not cover the required minimum distributions, whose age is set in law and is on the RMD page. The income-tax numbers announced a week earlier are on their own page, and the rule that lets someone 55 or older leave an employer and draw on its plan is on the rule of 55 page.

What’s worth keeping in mind

  • The 2027 401(k), 403(b), 457(b) and TSP limit is $25,500. The age-50 catch-up is $8,500; ages 60 to 63 get $11,750 instead.
  • The IRA limit is $7,500 and the IRA catch-up $1,100. Both are unchanged.
  • The Roth IRA range rises to $158,000–$173,000 single and $250,000–$260,000 joint.
  • The catch-up Roth threshold for 2027 is $155,000 of 2026 FICA wages from the plan sponsor.
  • These are limits, not targets. Whether a plan offers the higher catch-up, or any Roth option, is the plan’s decision.

The notice sets the ceilings. What a worker can do beneath them is up to the plan and the worker’s own income and age.
