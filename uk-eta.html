The UK’s £20 travel authorization: what Americans need before the flight

Britain now wants an Electronic Travel Authorisation from American visitors, checked by the airline before you board. The fee, the validity, and where the copycat sites are.

By Edward Silva · 5 minute read · Checked against the Home Office factsheet, UK Parliament and independent guides

A visit to Britain used to require a passport and an answer to a few questions at the border. Now it requires something before the flight. The UK’s Electronic Travel Authorisation, the ETA, is a digital permission linked to your passport that American visitors have to hold before they travel. It is live, it is cheap, and it is the kind of thing that can be sold to you by someone else at three times the price.

It is also not the same thing as the European Union’s ETIAS, which has not started. People mix them up constantly, and the mix-up is where most of the cost is.

The UK ETA, in numbers
£20The fee, per the Home Office’s April 2026 factsheet. Older guides list £16; they are out of date.
2 yearsHow long an ETA lasts, or until the passport expires, whichever comes first.
6 monthsThe longest stay it permits on any one visit. It allows multiple trips while valid.
Up to 3 working daysThe government’s stated processing window. Most decisions are reported to come within a day.
Each travelerEvery person, including a child, needs their own ETA. It is tied to a passport.
25 February 2026The date a UK Parliament statement says carriers must check for permission to travel before boarding.

What it is, and what it is not

An ETA is permission to travel to the UK. It is not a visa, and it is not a guarantee of entry. A border officer can still refuse a visitor who has one, and guides describe it as authorizing travel, not admission. It does not allow work. It is a screening step that moves the question of whether you may come from the border to before you leave.

It applies to Americans, Canadians, Australians and citizens of all 27 EU countries, among others. British and Irish citizens are exempt, including dual citizens, and a US-Irish dual national would travel on the Irish passport. Airlines now check. A UK Parliament written statement dated 25 February 2026 says carriers are required to verify, through automated checks against Home Office records, that a passenger holds permission to travel before they board. A traveler without one can be refused at the gate, which is a worse place to find out than a website.

The exemptions are narrow. People who already hold certain UK immigration permissions are exempt, as are some transit passengers. A visitor from the United States who is not British or Irish should assume the ETA applies, and check GOV.UK for the specific case. The ETA is linked to a passport and does not replace it. The six-month limit is a ceiling on any one visit, not a promise of how long a border officer will allow.

How to apply, and how long it takes

The Home Office’s factsheet points applicants to the UK ETA app, available from the Apple and Google stores, and guides add that an application can also be made through the government’s own GOV.UK pages. The application asks for a passport, a photograph and some questions, and the stated decision window is up to three working days. Reporting says most applications are decided within a day, which makes the sensible rule to apply well before the trip, and not at the airport.

The official fee is £20. Anything that costs more is a service fee for filling in the same form.

Where the extra charges come from

Several third-party websites advertise UK ETA services at a higher price, and they are not the official channel. One guide notes that they charge a service fee on top of the £20, and that paying more does not get a faster decision. The pattern is familiar from the ETIAS page: the real thing exists, a copy sits in front of it, and the copy looks official because it uses the same words. The check is the same too. The application is made in the government’s own app or on a GOV.UK address. A site that asks for a larger payment, or that promises an “express” decision, is not that.

The details that cause trouble

Because the ETA is tied to a passport, a new passport means a new ETA. A person who renews their passport between buying the ticket and flying needs to apply again for the new document. Every traveler needs their own, including a grandchild on the same trip, and there is no family application. And the passport used to apply must be the one the traveler flies on, which matters for someone who holds two.

Several commercial guides say Americans have needed an ETA since early 2025. This page does not repeat the exact date, because the official sources checked did not give it. What the official sources do show is that the requirement is in force and that airlines enforce it. One guide also describes an exemption for passengers who stay in the airside transit area without passing through UK border control and calls it temporary. That is not confirmed here and should be checked on GOV.UK before a connection.

Where this sits among the other changes

Europe’s fingerprint and photo checks have been running at Schengen borders since April, and the EU’s authorization, ETIAS, has no start date. The UK’s ETA is the one of the three that requires an action before an American flies to Britain now. Travel documents that go wrong at borders in other ways are on the passport page, and the broader planning for a long trip is on the big trip page.

What’s worth keeping in mind

  • American visitors need a UK ETA before they fly. It costs £20 and lasts two years or until the passport expires.
  • Each traveler, including children, needs their own. It is tied to the passport, so a new passport means a new ETA.
  • Apply through the UK ETA app or GOV.UK. Decisions can take up to three working days.
  • Airlines check for permission to travel before boarding. An ETA is not a visa and not a guarantee of entry.
  • Sites charging more than £20 are not the government.

The form takes a few minutes and the fee is small. The cost, when there is one, comes from the wrong website, or from finding out at the gate.
