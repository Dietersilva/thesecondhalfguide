Most Social Security calculators answer one question: **What might my monthly check be?**

That is useful, but it is not the whole retirement decision.

If you claim at 62 instead of 67, what happens to the amount you need to withdraw from savings? If you are still working, could the earnings test withhold part of the early checks? How long do you have to live before waiting catches up? What if you and your spouse claim at different ages? And what does age 65 mean for Medicare if you are not ready to claim Social Security yet?

We built **The Second Half Retirement Planner** to put those questions on one screen.

Start with the quick calculation. If you want more control, open Advanced customization for contributions, employer match, pensions, exact SSA estimates, investment-return assumptions, Medicare/HSA timing and more.

**Run your numbers:** https://thesecondhalfguide.com/retirement-strategy-model

The calculator is educational, not a recommendation. It does not ask for your name, Social Security number, account number or login. Financial entries are calculated in your browser and are not intended to be stored by The Second Half Guide.

## 60-second explainer script

“Should you take Social Security at 62, wait until 65, or hold off until 67? Most calculators show you three monthly checks and stop there. But that is not how retirement works. If you start earlier, you may take less from savings. If you are still working, the earnings test can affect what actually gets paid. If you wait, the later monthly benefit is larger, but you have to fund the years before it starts. Couples can claim at different ages. Medicare starts on its own timetable. So we built The Second Half Retirement Planner. Enter a few basics for a quick comparison, or open Advanced customization for savings, contributions, pensions, investment assumptions, exact SSA estimates and Medicare timing. It shows the monthly benefit, the break-even age, spending coverage and the projected portfolio under each path. It is an educational estimate, not a recommendation, and it does not ask for your Social Security number or account login. Try it free at TheSecondHalfGuide.com.”
